Conference History

The Tri-County Conference is a Missouri State High School Activities Association (MSHSAA) conference in Mid-Missouri, with member schools across Boone, Cole, Cooper, Miller, and Moniteau counties. It traces its roots to 1932, making it one of the longer-running high school conferences in the region.

Origins

Before the Tri-County existed, California, along with several other area schools, competed in the Little Ten Conference. That conference dissolved after the 1932–33 basketball season once Columbia and Jefferson City — by far the two largest schools in it — came to dominate the smaller schools too thoroughly for the conference to stay competitive.

In its place, the Tri-County Conference was organized in the fall of 1932, drawing its name from the three counties its founding schools sat in — Moniteau, Morgan, and Miller. Coverage in the Tipton Times from October 14, 1932 named the founding members as California, Clarksburg, Eldon, Tipton, and Versailles, competing not just in basketball, track, and tennis, but originally in debating, oratory, music, and declamation as well — a reminder that early 20th-century Missouri "athletic" conferences often doubled as academic and activity leagues. The first conference schedule began December 2, 1932.

Eldon and California are the only two schools that have belonged to the conference continuously since that first season in 1932.

Membership Through the Years

The conference has changed considerably over nine decades, growing, shrinking, and losing members to school consolidations and realignment as travel distances and school sizes shifted. Its most recent addition, Fulton, joined for the 2026–27 school year — the first new member since Boonville rejoined in 2018, and the conference's first expansion to eight teams in that stretch.

State Championships

Member schools have combined for state titles across a range of sports over the conference's history. For the 8 current members, every state title the school has ever won is included, even from years before it joined Tri-County (this is why Fulton and Boonville show up with championships despite being recent additions). For former members, only titles won while actually in the conference are included, to keep the list about what Tri-County produced rather than what those schools went on to do elsewhere.
